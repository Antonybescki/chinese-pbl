欢迎 · Huānyíng

Welcome to the Northern Capital

KA-ME-HA-ME-HA!
悟空老师 Wùkōng lǎoshī · Goku teaches this part

Beijing 北京 (Běijīng) literally means “northern capital.” For most of the last eight centuries this has been the seat of China’s emperors and governments, and it shows: golden palace roofs, grey-brick lanes and glass towers all share the same skyline.

It is a city to explore slowly — one gate, one courtyard and one bowl of noodles at a time.

Key highlights

  • The vibe: grand, proud and surprisingly relaxed — imperial history with a modern heartbeat.
  • Best way to explore: on foot and by bicycle through the hutongs, and by subway everywhere else.
  • Goal of this guide: to help you feel the soul of the city, from the Great Wall to a street-side breakfast.

02
长城 · Chángchéng

Dragon of Stone: The Great Wall

RASENGAN!
鸣人老师 Míngrén lǎoshī · Naruto teaches this part

No trip to Beijing is complete without standing on the Great Wall. The sections north of the city were rebuilt in stone and brick by the Ming dynasty, snaking along mountain ridges like a sleeping dragon. Each one offers a different mood — choose the one that fits you.

Badaling

八达岭

The most famous and best restored. Easy to reach, but busy.

Mutianyu

慕田峪

Fewer crowds, forested views, a cable car up and a toboggan down.

Jinshanling

金山岭

Wilder and quieter — the favourite of hikers and photographers.

💡 Quick fact · Did you know?

All the walls built by different dynasties add up to 21,196 km — more than half the distance around the Earth. And the old saying is a myth: the wall cannot be seen from space with the naked eye.

03
故宫 · Gùgōng

Inside the Forbidden City

I HAVE NO ENEMIES
托尔芬老师 Tuō’ěrfēn lǎoshī · Thorfinn teaches this part

For almost 500 years ordinary people were forbidden to enter this palace. Built between 1406 and 1420, it was home to 24 emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties.

Today it is the Palace Museum: about 980 buildings on 72 hectares, all aligned on Beijing’s north–south Central Axis.

Step-by-step route

  1. Meridian Gate 午门Enter from the south, as officials once did.
  2. Hall of Supreme Harmony 太和殿The largest hall, where emperors were crowned.
  3. Imperial Garden 御花园Old cypress trees, rockeries and pavilions.
  4. Jingshan Park 景山Climb the hill behind for the famous rooftop view.

💡 Colour code

Look up! Yellow roof tiles were reserved for the emperor, and red walls stand for good fortune. Legend says the palace has 9,999½ rooms — half a room fewer than heaven’s 10,000.

04
天坛 · Tiāntán

Heaven & Harmony: Temples and Gardens

HOLLOW PURPLE
五条老师 Wǔtiáo lǎoshī · Gojo teaches this part

Temple of Heaven

天坛 · Tiāntán

Here emperors prayed each year for a good harvest. The round Hall of Prayer for Good Harvests rises 38 metres on three marble terraces and was built entirely of wood, without a single nail. Come at sunrise: the park fills with locals practising tai chi, singing and playing cards.

Summer Palace

颐和园 · Yíhéyuán

The emperors’ lakeside escape from the summer heat. Stroll the Long Corridor — 728 metres of painted beams — cross the Seventeen-Arch Bridge and find the famous Marble Boat. Kunming Lake covers about three-quarters of the gardens; in winter it freezes into a giant skating rink.

💡 Did you know?

Round and square: the temple’s halls are round and their bases square, because ancient China pictured heaven as round and the earth as square. Whisper at the Echo Wall and a friend far along the wall can hear you.

05
胡同 · Hútòng

Old Beijing: Hutongs & Culture

SHATTER.
蓝染老师 Lánrǎn lǎoshī · Aizen teaches this part

Step away from the wide avenues into the hutongs — narrow grey-brick lanes lined with courtyard homes called 四合院 sìhéyuàn. Neighbours play chess on the doorstep and songbirds sing from cages in the trees.

Wander Nanluoguxiang 南锣鼓巷, circle the lakes of Houhai 后海 and listen for the old Drum and Bell Towers.

Exploration tips

  1. Look at the doorsStone drums, door studs and red couplets once showed a family’s rank and wishes.
  2. Remember these are homesKeep your voice down and always ask before taking photos of people.
  3. Catch a showSee Peking opera 京剧 (Jīngjù) or sip jasmine tea in a traditional teahouse.

💡 Local secret

Rent a bicycle or hop on a pedicab at dusk — when the lanterns come on and dinner smells drift out of every courtyard, the hutongs are at their most magical.

06
北京味道 · Běijīng wèidào

Taste and Reflect: The Flavour of Beijing

GUM-GUM PISTOL!
路飞老师 Lùfēi lǎoshī · Luffy teaches this part

Beijing food is hearty northern cooking: wheat instead of rice, roast meats, noodles and dumplings. Every dish tells a story of emperors and cold winters. Tap a dish to hear its Chinese name.

💡 Table manners

Say “好吃！” (hǎochī, delicious!) to make any cook smile. Never stand chopsticks upright in rice — it resembles funeral incense.

07
现代北京 · Xiàndài Běijīng

Tomorrow’s Capital: Modern Beijing

ANYWHERE DOOR!
哆啦A梦老师 Duōlā A mèng lǎoshī · Doraemon teaches this part

Beijing is not only ancient. Since the 2008 Olympics it has become a showcase of daring design, with bold new landmarks rising beside the old palaces.

Bird’s Nest

鸟巢

The National Stadium of the 2008 Olympics, wrapped in a tangle of steel “twigs.” Stunning when lit at night.

Water Cube

水立方

The bubble-skinned swimming centre next door; it became the “Ice Cube” for curling in 2022.

China Zun

中国尊

At 528 m, the tallest tower in the city. Its curved shape copies an ancient bronze wine vessel.

CCTV Headquarters

央视大楼

A giant folded loop of glass and steel that locals jokingly call “the Big Pants.”

798 Art District

798

A 1950s factory complex reborn as galleries, street art, design shops and cafés.

Daxing Airport

大兴机场

A starfish-shaped mega-airport opened in 2019, designed by Zaha Hadid Architects.

💡 Night owl tip

Walk the Olympic Green after dark for a free light show, then head to Sanlitun 三里屯 for cafés and shopping.

08
说中文 · Shuō Zhōngwén

Speak Like a Local: Survival Mandarin

WAKU WAKU!
阿尼亚老师 Āníyà lǎoshī · Anya teaches this part

Beijing is the home of standard Mandarin, 普通话 (Pǔtōnghuà). Even a few words will earn you big smiles. Tap any card to hear it.

The tones: one sound, five meanings

💡 The Beijing “r”

Beijingers love to curl the end of words with an “-r” sound, written 儿. So nǎlǐ (where) becomes nǎr, and yìdiǎn (a little) becomes yìdiǎnr. Try it — you will sound like a true local.

09
四季 · Sìjì

When to Go: Seasons & Festivals

♥♥♥♥♥♥♥♥♥♥♥♥♥♥MOON TIARA MAGIC!
月野兔老师 Yuèyě Tù lǎoshī · Usagi teaches this part

Beijing has four sharply different seasons, and each one changes the city completely. Whenever you come, there is a festival, a flavour and a view to match.

Spring

春 chūn
Mar – May

Blossom in the parks and mild, breezy days. Bring a light jacket.

Summer

夏 xià
Jun – Aug

Hot and humid with sudden rain. Start early and rest at midday.

Autumn

秋 qiū
Sep – Nov

Clear blue skies and red leaves. The best season to visit.

Winter

冬 dōng
Dec – Feb

Cold and dry. Fewer crowds, frozen lakes and hot pot weather.

春节 Spring Festival

Chūnjié · Jan / Feb

Chinese New Year. Temple fairs 庙会 fill the parks with lion dances, snacks and red lanterns.

元宵节 Lantern Festival

Yuánxiāojié · 15 days later

Glowing lantern displays and sweet rice balls end the New Year season.

中秋节 Mid-Autumn Festival

Zhōngqiūjié · Sep / Oct

Families share mooncakes and admire the full moon over Beihai Park.

国庆节 National Day

Guóqìngjié · 1 October

Flowers and flags fill Tiananmen Square. Expect big holiday crowds all week.

💡 Best time to visit

September and October bring the famous “Beijing autumn” — dry air, golden ginkgo trees and deep blue skies. Just avoid the first week of October, when all of China is on holiday.

Final checklist

  • Valid passport and the right visa for China checked.
  • Transport card or mobile payment app set up and ready.
  • Comfortable walking shoes packed — you will walk a lot.
  • Forbidden City and Great Wall tickets booked in advance.
  • Translation and map apps downloaded for offline use.
  • A few Mandarin phrases practised: 你好, 谢谢, 再见!

💡 Planning tip

The Forbidden City is closed on Mondays and tickets sell out — book online in advance and bring your passport, which is checked at the gate.
