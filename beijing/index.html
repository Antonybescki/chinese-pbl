01 欢迎 Welcome

Welcome to the northern capital

Goku 悟空老师

I'm Goku. Beijing means northern capital, and about 22 million people live here. That is a lot of energy. Stay close and we will walk it together.

Beijing 北京 (Běijīng) means northern capital. For most of the last eight centuries, emperors and governments ruled from here. You can see it: golden palace roofs, grey brick lanes and glass towers share one skyline.

Here is the trick this guide uses. A road called the Central Axis 中轴线 runs north to south through the middle of the city, about 7.8 km long. Almost every big sight sits on it, or close to it. So we will walk it, one stop at a time.

02 长城 Great Wall

The Great Wall, a dragon of stone

Naruto 鸣人老师

Naruto here. The wall north of Beijing was rebuilt by the Ming dynasty. All the walls together reach 21,196 km. That is a long run, even with a thousand clones.

No trip to Beijing is complete without standing on the Great Wall. The parts north of the city were rebuilt in stone and brick by the Ming dynasty, and they snake along mountain ridges like a sleeping dragon. Pick the section that fits your mood.

Badaling

八达岭

The most famous and best restored. Easy to reach, but busy.

Mutianyu

慕田峪

Fewer crowds and forest views. Ride a cable car up and a toboggan down.

Jinshanling

金山岭

Wilder and quieter. The favourite of hikers and photographers.

Did you know?

The old saying that you can see the wall from space is a myth. You cannot spot it with the naked eye.

03 故宫 Forbidden City

Inside the Forbidden City

Thorfinn 托尔芬老师

I am Thorfinn. Ordinary people were kept out of this palace for almost 500 years. Walk through it slowly. A good traveller does not need a sword, only patience.

Built between 1406 and 1420, this palace was home to 24 emperors of the Ming and Qing dynasties. Today it is the Palace Museum: about 980 buildings on 72 hectares, all lined up on the Central Axis.

Colour code

Yellow roof tiles were only for the emperor. Red walls bring good luck. Legend says the palace has 9,999 and a half rooms, half a room fewer than heaven.

Closed on Mondays. Book tickets online and bring your passport.

The walking route

  1. Meridian Gate 午门Enter from the south, as officials once did.
  2. Hall of Supreme Harmony 太和殿The biggest hall, where emperors were crowned.
  3. Imperial Garden 御花园Old cypress trees, rockeries and pavilions.
  4. Jingshan Park 景山Climb the hill behind for the famous rooftop view.

04 天坛 Temple of Heaven

Temple of Heaven and Summer Palace

Gojo 五条老师

Yo, Gojo here. The Hall of Prayer was built without a single nail and it has stood for six hundred years. Almost as impressive as me. Almost.

Temple of Heaven 天坛

Tiāntán

Emperors came here every year to pray for a good harvest. The round Hall of Prayer rises 38 metres on three marble terraces and has no nails at all. Visit at sunrise, when locals practise tai chi, sing and play cards in the park.

Summer Palace 颐和园

Yíhéyuán

The emperors escaped the summer heat here. Walk the Long Corridor, 728 metres of painted beams, cross the Seventeen-Arch Bridge and find the Marble Boat. In winter the lake freezes into a huge skating rink.

Round and square

The halls are round and their bases square, because ancient China pictured heaven as round and the earth as square. Whisper at the Echo Wall and a friend far along it can hear you.

05 胡同 Hutongs

Old Beijing, lane by lane

Aizen 蓝染老师

I am Aizen. These lanes look simple. Every door hides a story, so look closely. Things are rarely what they seem.

Step off the wide avenues into the hutongs 胡同: narrow grey brick lanes lined with courtyard homes called sìhéyuàn 四合院. Neighbours play chess on the doorstep and songbirds sing from cages in the trees.

Wander Nanluoguxiang 南锣鼓巷, circle the lakes of Houhai 后海 and listen for the old Drum and Bell Towers.

Local secret

Rent a bicycle or hop on a pedicab at dusk. When the lanterns come on and dinner smells drift out of every courtyard, the hutongs are at their best.

Before you go in

  1. Look at the doorsStone drums, door studs and red couplets once showed a family's rank and wishes.
  2. Remember these are homesKeep your voice down and ask before you photograph people.
  3. Catch a showSee Peking opera 京剧 (Jīngjù) or sip jasmine tea in a teahouse.

06 美食 Food

The flavour of Beijing

Luffy 路飞老师

I'm Luffy! I'm going to eat every dish in Beijing. Peking duck, noodles, dumplings, hot pot. Meat! Let's eat!

Beijing cooking is hearty northern food: wheat instead of rice, roast meats, noodles and dumplings. Every dish tells a story about emperors and cold winters.

鸭

Peking duck 北京烤鸭 Běijīng kǎoyā

Glossy roast duck carved at the table and wrapped in thin pancakes with spring onion and sweet bean sauce.

面

Zhajiang noodles 炸酱面 zhájiàngmiàn

Thick noodles tossed in a rich fried soybean paste with fresh cucumber. Beijing comfort food.

饼

Jianbing 煎饼 jiānbing

The breakfast of the city: a hot crêpe with egg, herbs, chilli sauce and a crunchy cracker.

锅

Mutton hotpot 涮羊肉 shuàn yángròu

Paper-thin lamb swished in a copper pot of bubbling broth. Perfect in winter.

饺

Dumplings 饺子 jiǎozi

Boiled parcels of meat and vegetables, eaten with vinegar. Families make them together at New Year.

糖

Tanghulu 糖葫芦 tánghúlu

Hawthorn berries on a stick, dipped in clear, crackling sugar.

Table manners

Say 好吃! (hǎochī, delicious) to make any cook smile. Never stand chopsticks upright in rice, because it looks like incense for the dead.

07 现代 Modern Beijing

Tomorrow's capital

Doraemon 哆啦A梦老师

I'm Doraemon, a robot cat from the future. Modern Beijing already looks like my century. Hold on tight.

Beijing is not only old. Since the 2008 Olympics it has filled with daring buildings that stand right beside the old palaces.

Bird's Nest 鸟巢

National Stadium

The Olympic stadium wrapped in a tangle of steel twigs. Beautiful when lit at night.

Water Cube 水立方

National Aquatics Centre

A swimming centre with a bubble skin. It became the Ice Cube for curling in 2022.

China Zun 中国尊

528 metres

The tallest tower in the city. Its curved shape copies an ancient bronze wine vessel.

CCTV Headquarters 央视大楼

The Big Pants

A folded loop of glass and steel that locals joke about.

798 Art District

798艺术区

An old 1950s factory complex, now galleries, street art and cafés.

Daxing Airport 大兴机场

Starfish shape

A huge starfish-shaped airport that opened in 2019.

Night owl tip

Walk the Olympic Green after dark for a free light show, then head to Sanlitun 三里屯 for cafés and shopping.

08 交通 Transit

Moving through the city

Guts 格斯老师

I'm Guts. I have walked a long road carrying a heavy sword. In Beijing the subway carries you instead. Just keep moving forward.

Getting around is part of the trip. The Beijing Subway 地铁 (dìtiě) is one of the biggest metro systems on Earth. Signs and announcements come in Chinese and English, and a ride costs a few yuan.

Above ground, join the river of bicycles. Shared bikes wait on every corner and the old city is flat. Leaving town? High-speed trains 高铁 reach Shanghai, 1,318 km away, in about four and a half hours.

800+km of subway track

Traveller tip

Get a Yikatong 一卡通 transport card or set up a mobile payment app as soon as you land. One tap works on subways and buses across the whole city. Avoid 7 to 9 am and 5 to 7 pm if you can.

09 公园 Parks and Hills

Beyond the walls: parks, hills and tombs

Eren Yeager 艾伦老师

Eren Yeager here. Beijing's parks are clean, so keep them that way. Climb the hill, enjoy the view, and carry your rubbish out.

When the city feels too big, do what Beijingers do and head for the trees. Imperial gardens and forested hills are a short subway or bus ride away.

香

Fragrant Hills 香山 Xiāngshān

Hillsides that blaze red and gold with autumn leaves every October.

海

Beihai Park 北海公园 Běihǎi

A thousand-year-old imperial garden. Row a boat around the White Dagoba.

陵

Ming Tombs 十三陵 Shísānlíng

The valley where 13 Ming emperors rest, reached by a road of stone animals.

景

Jingshan Park 景山 Jǐngshān

A small hill with the best sunset view over the golden roofs of the Forbidden City.

Hiker's checklist
  • Pack plenty of water, sun protection and snacks.
  • Check the weather and air quality before you set off.
  • Wear shoes with grip. Old steps are steep and uneven.
  • Carry your rubbish out and respect wildlife.

10 中文 Language

Speak like a local

Anya 阿尼亚老师

Anya likes Chinese! Anya will teach you. Say it with Anya: nǐ hǎo! Waku waku!

Beijing is the home of standard Mandarin, 普通话 (Pǔtōnghuà). Even a few words earn big smiles. Read the pinyin aloud and watch the tone marks.

One sound, five meanings. Tap a tone.

The Beijing r

Beijingers love to curl the end of words with an r sound, written 儿. So nǎlǐ (where) becomes nǎr, and yìdiǎn (a little) becomes yìdiǎnr. Try it and you will sound like a local.

11 四季 Seasons and Festivals

When to go

Usagi 月野兔老师

I'm Usagi! Beijing has four seasons and lots of festivals. In the name of the Moon, I will show you the best time to visit.

Beijing has four sharply different seasons, and each one changes the city. Whenever you come, there is a festival, a flavour and a view to match.

春

Spring

Mar to May

Blossom in the parks and mild, breezy days. Bring a light jacket.

夏

Summer

Jun to Aug

Hot and humid with sudden rain. Start early and rest at midday.

秋

Autumn

Sep to Nov

Clear blue skies and red leaves. The best season to visit.

冬

Winter

Dec to Feb

Cold and dry. Fewer crowds, frozen lakes and hotpot weather.

春节 Spring Festival

Jan or Feb

Chinese New Year. Temple fairs fill the parks with lion dances, snacks and red lanterns.

元宵节 Lantern Festival

15 days later

Glowing lantern displays and sweet rice balls end the New Year season.

中秋节 Mid-Autumn Festival

Sep or Oct

Families share mooncakes and watch the full moon over Beihai Park.

国庆节 National Day

1 October

Flowers and flags fill Tiananmen Square. Expect big holiday crowds all week.

Best time to visit

September and October bring the famous Beijing autumn: dry air, golden ginkgo trees and deep blue skies. Skip the first week of October, when all of China is on holiday.
